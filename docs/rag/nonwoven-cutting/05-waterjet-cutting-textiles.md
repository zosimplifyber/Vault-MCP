# Waterjet Cutting of Fabrics and Textiles — TechniWaterjet

Source: TechniWaterjet. https://www.techniwaterjet.com/fabric-textile-cutting/

## Why waterjet matters for this project

Waterjet is the only contour-capable method in this collection that is both **heat-free**
and **indifferent to fibre chemistry**. For a non-thermoplastic web, where ultrasonic
loses its force advantage and laser chars rather than fuses, waterjet stays fully
applicable.

The cost is that the part comes out wet.

## Pure waterjet versus abrasive waterjet on textiles

TechniWaterjet describes waterjet cutting as using "a high-pressure stream of water,
sometimes mixed with abrasives, to cut fabrics."

**For textiles and nonwovens, use pure waterjet with no abrasive.** Abrasive garnet is for
metal, stone and thick composite. Introduced into a fibrous web it shreds the material
rather than cutting it, and embeds particulate in the fibre.

Fabric work sits at the low end of this range. The high-pressure series exist for metal and
stone.

## Advantages of waterjet on fabric

- **No heat-affected zone.** No melting, no charring, no scorch line, no thermal
  degradation of a heat-sensitive fibre or finish.
- **No blade pressure and no physical contact**, therefore "no material distortion" and no
  draw-in of the web toward the cut.
- The "fabric's properties, like weave and texture, remain unaltered."
- Suited to "delicate or heat-sensitive materials like silk, lace, or even leather."
- Environmentally clean: "does not produce harmful fumes or byproducts."
- One machine handles textiles alongside composites, plastics and metals.

## What TechniWaterjet does not publish

The page gives no data on nozzle or orifice sizes, kerf width, cutting speeds, ply
stacking, or which of its models are used for fabric. These are the numbers a trial has to
establish.

## Open questions for a waterjet trial on a foam-formed cellulosic web

These require a vendor trial, not further searching.

| Open question for waterjet cutting of a nonwoven web | Why this question matters for a foam-formed cellulosic sheet |
|---|---|
| Kerf width at pure-waterjet pressure | Sets minimum feature size and nesting spacing |
| Whether the jet blows through or distorts a low-basis-weight web | A lofty low-density sheet may not survive the jet at all |
| What backing or vacuum support is required | Porous low-density material needs support the vendor may not stock |
| Wetting depth and drying time after the cut | Adds a drying operation to the process chain and a cost per part |
| Dimensional recovery after wet-then-dry | **The one to watch.** A water-formed or foam-formed sheet may re-swell when wetted, then shrink unpredictably on drying, so the cut dimension is not the final dimension |
| Whether plies can be stacked without delamination or edge taper | Determines whether waterjet can ever be more than a single-ply process here |

**Dimensional recovery is the critical unknown.** Every other waterjet drawback is a cost.
Re-swelling would be a correctness problem: it would mean the method cannot hold tolerance
on this material class regardless of how well the cut itself performs.
