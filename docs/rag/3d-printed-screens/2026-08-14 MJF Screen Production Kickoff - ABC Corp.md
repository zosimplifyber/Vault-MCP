## MJF wall thickness and warping on 3D-printed screens

ABC Corp's engineers reviewed the Simplifyber screen files and found them
feasible for MJF printing. Their one callout was wall thickness: the flatter
screen part is specified at 1 mm, and the recommended wall thickness for the MJF
platform is 2–3 mm.

ABC Corp judged the 1 mm wall acceptable anyway, for these reasons specific to
this screen: the geometry is not long or wonky, the part is circular rather than
elongated, and it occupies only the smaller end of the build box. With a full
cooldown allowed after the print, ABC Corp's engineers did not expect warping to
be a significant problem at 1 mm. Extending the wall to 2–3 mm would still be the
most ideal outcome if there is room in the design.

Warping on MJF parts is driven by thin, long, elongated geometry, and rectangular
parts are the worst case. Circular parts such as these screens are the favorable
case.

Simplifyber is not married to the 1 mm wall thickness and controls that dimension
fully. The 1 mm value is kept because of a working theory that it helps the
Simplifyber forming process, but that theory is still being actively developed.
ABC Corp offered to run a sample trial comparing wall thicknesses.

## Warping tolerance is relaxed by how Simplifyber mounts the screens

Warping matters less for these screens than it would for a typical MJF part,
because Simplifyber's screens only need an approximately correct surface profile
— the screen is bent into its final shape on installation. Simplifyber is not
trying to hold a tight profile tolerance on the as-printed screen. ABC Corp
noted this as useful background they had not previously had.

## MJF build box size at ABC Corp

ABC Corp's MJF build box is approximately 15 × 11.5 × 15 (inches). The
Simplifyber screens in this first order sit on the smaller side of that build
volume, which is part of why the 1 mm wall was judged acceptable.

## Powder clearing on perforated MJF screens

Powder clearing after the print has been the failure point with previous vendors
for Simplifyber's 3D-printed screens, and it was the specific risk Zak Olech
raised about the small bowl feature.

ABC Corp's assessment is the opposite of the prior experience: because the screen
is covered in perforation holes, clearing the loose powder is easier, not harder.
ABC Corp saw no powder-clearing difficulty with the small bowl geometry. How ABC
Corp actually approaches de-powdering was flagged as worth understanding later,
since other vendors have struggled with it.

## Material selection — PA12 versus PA11 for MJF screens

Simplifyber will proceed with PA12 for this first order, while keeping PA11 in
mind for later trials.

PA12 is the stiff, sturdy polyamide and accounts for roughly 90% of overall 3D
printing volume. PA11 costs a little more, has more give, and wins on impact
resistance. ABC Corp has supplied screens in both PA12 and PA11; the choice comes
down to preference and the actual application. On large thermoforming machines
that pump a lot of pressure through the screen, many customers elect PA11. For
the Simplifyber application, ABC Corp expected PA12 to hold up fine.

Simplifyber's reasoning for PA12 is that its process does not load the screen the
way a high-pressure thermoformer would. The KFT 90 press (transcribed as "KFC 90"
/ "key pool" in the raw transcript) presses free and then dumps the part off, so
there is no direct heat exposure on the screen, and Simplifyber runs a support
structure behind the screen that should mitigate much of the incoming force. The
screen's duty is essentially wet: a fiber mat or "cake" is built on it.

## Moisture absorption requirement for Simplifyber screens

Simplifyber's binding material requirement for these 3D-printed screens is that
the material must not absorb moisture excessively, because the screens operate in
a wet environment. Beyond moisture uptake and the ability to be vapor smoothed,
Simplifyber had no particular material concerns about the polyamides ABC Corp
works with. Vapor smoothing was noted as helping on the moisture front.

## Vendor onboarding and payment terms with ABC Corp

Getting set up in ABC Corp's system is the gating step before the order can be
placed and sent to production; ABC Corp called new-customer onboarding the
biggest hurdle in new business. Christian will send a fillable onboarding link
immediately after the call.

The ABC Corp onboarding form collects payment preference, AP contacts, and
tax-exempt status, and requires a W-9. If Simplifyber wants net terms or ACH
payment, ABC Corp additionally requires a credit application listing three trade
references, and ABC Corp's finance team runs a background and credit check.
Paying by credit card avoids the credit application and is the straightforward
option for a first order.

ABC Corp will also re-review the Simplifyber screen files with its engineering
team before the order is actually placed.

## Shipping arrangements for the ABC Corp screen order

ABC Corp offers aggressive shipping rates on the strength of its daily shipment
volume. Simplifyber can either have ABC Corp prepay and add shipping to the
invoice, or ship on a Simplifyber-preferred carrier account. Ground, second day,
and next day are all available; the method is Simplifyber's choice.
